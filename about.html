BIOGRAPHY & PRINCIPLES

Engineering Practical Systems with Discipline.

I am a Computer Engineering student who believes in learning by building. From real-time multiplayer protocols to retrieval-augmented intelligence, here is what drives my work.

The Background

Originally from Jhapa, Nepal, I moved to Kathmandu to pursue my Bachelor in Computer Engineering at Himalaya College of Engineering, Tribhuvan University. My curiosity has always centered around the machinery behind computing: how bytes flow across networks, how distributed components maintain consistent state, and how intelligent models can be systematically harnessed to solve real-world problems.

I do not believe in exaggerating claims or calling myself a senior engineer. I am an enthusiastic, disciplined student developer who puts in the hours to understand how things work under the hood—from configuring socket connections in Node.js to running quantized LLMs on resource-constrained local hardware.

"Building software is not about accumulating superficial tools; it is about choosing the right architecture, writing dependable logic, and creating interfaces people can actually use."

Applied Intelligence & Backend Systems

My hands-on projects span across several technical disciplines:

  • Real-Time Multiplayer: Co-engineering Quiz Royale with a 4-person team, designing WebSocket synchronization and server-authoritative game state.
  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): Implementing document chunking, overlap preservation, local vector embeddings, and MongoDB Atlas Vector Search to answer questions grounded in domain literature without hallucinations.
  • Offline Inference: Experimenting with local model inference (GGUF formats) to balance response coherence with hardware latency constraints.
  • Speech Processing: Creating unified speech-to-text and text-to-speech tools that streamline natural vocal interaction.

Leadership & Campus Life

Engineering doesn't happen in a vacuum. During July 2026, I had the honor of controlling and conducting the Himalaya Exhibition 2026. Managing an event of this scale involved coordinating with faculty, guiding a team of 19 student staff members, and ensuring that hundreds of visitors and project participants enjoyed a structured, engaging, and welcoming environment.
